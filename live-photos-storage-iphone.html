Are Live Photos taking up space on your iPhone?

Yes: a Live Photo keeps a short video and its audio alongside the still image, so it holds more data than the same shot without motion. To stop new ones, set Live to Off in Camera and turn on Preserve Settings; to reclaim space from old ones, delete those you no longer want and clear them from Recently Deleted.

How much space does a Live Photo use?

Apple does not publish a fixed size, and the honest answer varies by camera, scene and format. What Apple does document is the structure: a Live Photo captures what happens just before and after the shutter, including sound, and its asset includes a separate video component. So treat any single “megabytes per Live Photo” figure you read online as an estimate, not a rule.

You can measure your own result instead. Open Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, and note the figure for Photos before a session. Check it again after you have cleared Recently Deleted. The screen can take a little time to update, and with Optimize iPhone Storage turned on the local change may be smaller than you expect, because the phone may hold smaller versions of many items. Our guide to storage that stays full explains why.

Stop new Live Photos in Camera

This changes future shots only. Existing Live Photos stay as they are.

  • iOS 27: In Photo mode, tap the Camera Controls button or swipe up from the mode list, tap Live, then choose Off.
  • iOS 26: Open the same controls and tap Live until it reads Live Off.
  • iOS 17 and 18: Tap the Live Photo button at the top of the camera screen so that a slash appears through it.

To make the choice stick, go to Settings, Camera, Preserve Settings and check that Live Photo is on. Apple describes this setting as saving your last Live Photo choice, so it is not reset the next time you open Camera. It is on by default, but it is worth checking if Live keeps coming back. iOS 26 and 27 also offer an Auto option, which is not the same as Off. Live Photos are not available while ProRAW or HEIF Max is on.

What each action actually changes

Several controls sound as though they would save space. Apple only documents what they do to the photo, so this table separates that from storage.

ActionWhat happensStorage effect
Set Live to Off in CameraNew shots are ordinary stillsLess added from now on; nothing freed today
Edit, tap Live, turn Live offShows a still of the key photo; Revert to Original can undo editsNo saving documented; do not count on one
Trim or mute in EditChanges what playsNo saving documented
More, then Save as VideoMakes a short video from the Live PhotoAdds a new video to your library
Delete the Live PhotoMoves the still and motion to Recently DeletedFreed once it leaves Recently Deleted, by you or after 30 days

The second row causes the most confusion. Turning Live off is reversible, which is useful, but a reversible edit is not evidence that the motion data has gone. If space is the goal, deletion is the only action here that Apple clearly ties to removing the item.

Find old Live Photos in Apple Photos

You do not need another app to see them. On iOS 26 and 27, tap Collections, scroll down, tap Media Types, then Live Photos. On iOS 18, scroll down in Photos and tap Media Types. On iOS 17, tap Albums and look under Media Types. Tap Select to choose several at once, then tap the Delete button. For a small batch of obvious rejects, this is all you need.

Deleted items go to Recently Deleted for 30 days. With iCloud Photos on, a deletion applies to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, so read the iCloud deletion guide before a larger pass. Keep a separate backup of anything irreplaceable.

Which Live Photos are worth keeping?

Play the photo before you decide, because the thumbnail hides the part you might care about. Touch and hold it to hear the sound and see the movement. A child’s first words, a pet’s reaction or a laugh just after the shutter can be the whole point. A receipt, a parking-level sign or a whiteboard gained nothing from its motion.

Repeated moments are the easiest place to start. If you took six Live Photos of the same view, keep the one you like and let the others go. The similar versus duplicate guide offers a way to compare them. If you like a photo but not its moment of motion, you can change its key photo in Edit, which picks the frame shown as the still.

Review Live Photos one at a time in Swipe & Delete

Photo Cleaner: Swipe & Delete gathers them into a single queue, which helps when a grid makes every shot look alike. On Home, go to Browse library, then All Categories, then Live Photos under Photo types. Cards play the motion, so you can judge the real moment. Swipe right to keep or left to mark for removal, and use Undo if you change your mind.

Nothing is deleted until you open the Trash Bin, select items and tap Delete. Recover takes an item back out of the Trash Bin. After you confirm in the iOS dialog, items move to Recently Deleted, where the 30-day rule above applies. Three limits are worth knowing. The app does not convert a Live Photo to a still: it keeps or removes the whole item. Its storage figures are estimates, so use iPhone Storage for the real result. And with Limited Access it can only see the photos you have shared with it, as our Limited Access guide explains. The free allowance is one-time; beyond it, Pro is optional and Apple Photos remains free.

A worked plan for a large collection

Suppose Media Types shows 1,200 Live Photos. That number is only an example, so use your own. At 50 decisions per session, the collection takes 24 sessions (1,200 ÷ 50). At three sessions a week, that is 8 weeks (24 ÷ 3). You may stop long before that, because repeated shots may be easy to decide on quickly.

  1. Turn Live off first if you do not want more of them.
  2. Record the Photos figure from iPhone Storage.
  3. Review one batch of 50, keeping anything you are unsure about.
  4. Check your selections before confirming deletion.
  5. Clear Recently Deleted only when you are sure, then compare the figure.

If two or three sessions barely change the figure, Live Photos are not your main storage problem, and large videos may be a better place to look.

Questions, answered.

Do Live Photos take up more storage than normal photos?

They hold more data for the same shot, because Apple stores a video component and audio alongside the still. Apple does not publish a fixed size, so measure the change in iPhone Storage.

Does turning off Live on an existing photo free up space?

Do not count on it. Apple documents that the photo becomes a still of its key photo, and edits can be reverted, but it does not document a storage saving. Deleting unwanted items is the clear route.

How do I keep Live Photos off permanently?

Set Live to Off in Camera, then go to Settings, Camera, Preserve Settings and make sure Live Photo is on. That saves your last choice instead of resetting it.

Can Swipe & Delete turn Live Photos into still photos?

No. It helps you review them and keep or remove each whole item. To make a still of a photo you keep, use the Live control in the Edit screen of Apple Photos.
