How to find and review large videos on iPhone

Check Settings → General → iPhone Storage, then open videos in Photos and swipe up or tap Info to inspect file details. Prioritize videos by their actual size and usefulness rather than guessing from how many thumbnails you see.

Check where the space is going first

Open iPhone Storage and let the usage view load before deciding that photos are the problem. The useful question is which category deserves your attention. A photo-library cleanup is a sensible project when your saved media is substantial. If another app accounts for most of the usage, reviewing hundreds of pictures may do little for the immediate concern. Let the actual storage view set your next task.

Write down the available space before you begin, along with the goal: enough room for a recording, an update, or daily use. This is a baseline for your own comparison, not a promise of a particular result. Avoid turning a rough estimate into a precise deadline. Your device can have other activity during the session, so treat a later reading as context rather than a controlled experiment.

Inspect the individual files

Find the Videos media-type collection in Photos and open a candidate. Swipe up or use Info to view details such as file size. Apple’s information guide documents that view. Start with clips you remember being long or no longer useful, but confirm the size before making assumptions. The visible length, resolution and contents help you understand a file; the number of clips by itself does not.

For a small library, write a short candidate list with the event, approximate size and keep-or-review decision. This avoids jumping between unrelated recordings. For a large library, focus first on an obvious group such as obsolete screen recordings or repeated attempts at a demonstration. You are looking for a manageable set of decisions, not inspecting every video before doing anything useful.

Watch before you choose

Play important candidates, including the middle and ending when appropriate. A recording can begin with an uninteresting setup and end with the only good view of a meaningful moment. Listen when the audio might matter. A shaky video may preserve a person’s voice, a performance or a useful explanation. The best deletion candidate is the one you know you no longer need, not automatically the biggest file.

Choose different rules for different kinds of recording. For work demonstrations, keep the final useful version and assess failed attempts. For family moments, consider whether shorter alternatives actually preserve the same event. For temporary tutorials, check whether the recording is still your only reference. When the rule is clear, review becomes faster without depending on indiscriminate selection or a promised amount of reclaimed space.

Verify separate copies when preserving originals

If you plan to remove a video after saving it elsewhere, check the copy before deletion. Open it at the destination, watch enough to confirm it is complete, and make sure you know how to find it again. Merely seeing a filename is not a complete verification. For a particularly important recording, give the copy check its own session instead of doing it while rushing through removals.

Consider who is responsible for the copy and whether the destination has its own retention policy. A file shared with someone else may be convenient without being a dependable personal archive. Write down the location of the recording and the reason you chose it. If you have not confirmed a copy you trust, keep the original candidate until you can. Extra review time is reasonable for an irreplaceable moment.

Use the app for a focused review

Photo Cleaner: Swipe & Delete includes large-video review with file details. Use it to identify candidates and make deliberate choices about what belongs in your library. Review the selected items before confirming deletion. Keep any recording whose contents or copy status you have not checked. This app’s role here is media review and cleanup; do not expect it to clear system files or other apps’ caches.

Build a short list around one purpose, such as removing obsolete screen recordings. Once that list is done, stop and reassess your storage need. If you already have sufficient room, there is no reason to continue removing meaningful videos just because more candidates remain. A useful workflow has a stopping condition that follows your goal. The camera roll plan helps define bounded sessions.

Make future recordings easier to manage

Before recording a long event, decide what you actually need to capture and where the result will live. Afterward, review accidental starts, abandoned attempts and redundant takes while you remember them. A clear name or note about the event can also make future decisions easier. The habit matters more than making every recording as short as possible; some moments deserve the whole clip.

When checking the outcome, give device storage time to update and separate your review decisions from final removal. With iCloud Photos enabled, deletion synchronizes rather than preserving an untouched cloud original. Read the iCloud safety guide before relying on a synced copy. The most useful result is enough room for your next task while the recordings you care about remain accessible.

Questions, answered.

Can Apple Photos show a video’s file size?

Open the video, then swipe up or tap Info. Apple documents file size among the metadata details available in Photos.

Is the longest video always the largest?

Do not assume so. Check actual file details before ranking candidates, and decide usefulness separately from size.

Does this app compress my videos?

This guide describes video review and deletion. Compression is not a feature promised by Photo Cleaner: Swipe & Delete on this site.
